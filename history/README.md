# Literary history and prize lists (Part 1)

About 8 Part 1 items are literary history and about 6 are prize items,
per the 2026 Invitational A test.

## Prize lists: what is in scope at each meet

Each meet tests only a window of Pulitzer years. The Nobel Prize in
Literature is tested in full (1901 to the present) at every meet.

| Meet | Pulitzer Fiction | Pulitzer Poetry | Pulitzer Drama | Nobel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Invitational A | 1918-1940 | 1922-1942 | 1918-1938 | 1901-2025 |
| Invitational B | 1941-1967 | 1943-1963 | 1939-1961 | 1901-2025 |
| District | 1968-1991 | 1964-1983 | 1962-1985 | 1901-2025 |
| Region | 1992-2012 | 1984-2001 | 1987-2006 | 1901-2025 |
| State | 2013-2026 | 2002-2026 | 2007-2026 | 1901-2026 |

So the study order is fixed by the calendar: the earliest Pulitzer decades
first, the newest last. Nobel laureates are a season-long drip.

Sources named on the reading list:
- pulitzer.org/prize-winners-by-category/219 (Fiction)
- pulitzer.org/prize-winners-by-category/224 (Poetry)
- pulitzer.org/prize-winners-by-category/218 (Drama)
- nobelprize.org/prizes/lists/all-nobel-prizes-in-literature/

Save them here as `nobel.csv`, `pulitzer-fiction.csv`, `pulitzer-poetry.csv`,
`pulitzer-drama.csv` with columns `year,winner,work,note`. Do not build
these lists from memory.

## Prize item styles (from the 2026 Invitational A test)

- Recipient of a given year's prize for a named work ("the 1926 Pulitzer
  Prize for Poetry for her collection *What's O'Clock*").
- Nobel recipient identified by the citation text ("for the power, honesty
  and deep-felt emotions of his dramatic works").
- Author with multiple prizes ("the Pulitzer Prize for Drama for 1920,
  1922, 1928, and 1957").
- Author identification by career description, where the prize is
  incidental (Steinbeck and *The Grapes of Wrath*, 1940).

## Literary history: what is tested

Period and age names as Harmon defines them, with dates and signature
figures. The most-tested history answers since 2009 (from
`terms/most-tested-terms.md`) include: Colonial Period, Graveyard School,
Beat Generation, Transcendentalism, Theater of the Absurd, Augustan Age,
Restoration Age, Lost Generation, Federalist Age, Harlem Renaissance,
Edwardian Age, Neoclassic Period, Knickerbocker Group, Realistic Period,
Great Awakening, Bloomsbury Group, Cockney School, Bluestockings.

Item styles:
- A list of authors: which period or group are they?
- A description of a period's traits: which period?
- NOT items: which of these is not an age within the English Renaissance?
- First-works items: the first English translation of Boethius, Bede,
  Caedmon's Hymn, therefore the Old English Period.

See `history/periods.md` for the per-period notes to fill in.

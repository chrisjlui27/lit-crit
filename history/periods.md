# Literary periods

Use Harmon's period names and dates exactly; items are worded from the
Handbook's "Outline of Literary History" and period entries. Fill in each
section as it comes up. Keep to authors likely on a test: canonical,
anthologized, or prize-winning.

For each: Harmon's dates, defining traits, five to ten major authors with
one signature work each, and the glossary terms attached to the period.

## English periods

### Old English Period (to 1100)
Bede, Caedmon, Cynewulf, *Beowulf*, Alfred's Boethius.

### Middle English Period (1100-1500)
Anglo-Norman Period; Chaucer, Langland, the Gawain poet, Malory.

### English Renaissance (1500-1660)
Early Tudor Age, Elizabethan Age, Jacobean Age, Caroline Age,
Commonwealth Interregnum. Metaphysical and Cavalier poets.

### Neoclassic Period (1660-1798)
Restoration Age, Augustan Age, Age of Johnson. Scriblerus Club, Graveyard
School, Bluestockings.

### Romantic Period (1798-1870)
Lake School, Cockney School, Pre-Raphaelites; Early Victorian Age.

### Realistic Period (1870-1914)
Late Victorian, Edwardian Age.

### Modernist Period (1914-1965)
Georgian Age, Bloomsbury Group, Angry Young Men.

### Postmodern Period (1965-)

## American periods

### Colonial Period (1607-1765)
Bradstreet, Taylor, Mather, Edwards; the Great Awakening.

### Revolutionary and Early National Period (1765-1830)
Federalist Age; Franklin, Freneau, Wheatley, Brackenridge, Tyler, Irving;
Knickerbocker Group.

### Romantic Period (1830-1865)
Transcendentalism; Hawthorne, Poe, Emerson, Thoreau, Melville, Whitman,
Dickinson.

### Realistic Period (1865-1900)
Twain, Howells, James; local color.

### Naturalistic and Symbolistic Period (1900-1930)
Crane, Norris, Dreiser, London; Imagism, Lost Generation, Harlem
Renaissance.

### Period of Conformity and Criticism (1930-1960) and after
Fugitives, Southern Renaissance, Beat Generation, Confessional poets,
Black Mountain, New York School, Postmodern.

## Movements often named in items
Imagism, Bloomsbury, Lost Generation, Southern Gothic, Theater of the
Absurd, Theatre of Cruelty, Angry Young Men, Black Mountain, New York
School, Magical Realism, Existentialism, Naturalism, Expressionism,
Surrealism, Aestheticism.

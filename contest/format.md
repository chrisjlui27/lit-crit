# UIL Literary Criticism: format, scoring, strategy

Verified against the 2026 Invitational A test and key in `resources/`.
Items still marked `[verify]` come from memory or secondary sources.

## The exam

90 minutes. 65 multiple-choice items (five options, A-E) and one required
essay.

| Part | What it tests | Items | Points each | Max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Knowledge of Literary Terms and of Literary History | 30 | 1 | 30 |
| 2 | The UIL Reading List | 20 | 2 | 40 |
| 3 | Ability in Literary Criticism (unseen passages) | 15 | 2 | 30 |
| 4 | Required Tie-Breaking Essay | 1 | 0 | 0 |

Maximum objective score 100. No deduction for wrong answers `[verify]`, so
every item should be answered.

## The essay is required

From the test instructions: contestants who do not write an essay are
disqualified even if they are not involved in a tie, and an essay that does
not show a sincere effort to discuss the assigned topic is disqualified.
Three sheets of paper are provided. The expected length is the Handbook's
"moderately brief discussion of a restricted topic": more than a few
sentences.

Judging criteria, in the order UIL lists them:

1. The degree to which the instructions have been followed.
2. The quality of the critical insight offered in response to the selection.
3. The overall effectiveness of the written discussion.
4. The grammatical correctness of the essay.

UIL's note to judges: critical insight is more important than prose style.
Objective scores are never altered by the essay; it only reorders ties.

## What each part actually contains

Based on the 2026 Invitational A test.

Part 1 (30 items):
- About 16 glossary terms, worded from Harmon's definitions. Several are
  NOT items ("Not a form of pattern poem is...").
- About 8 literary-history items: named periods and ages (Old English
  Period, Edwardian Age, Revolutionary and Early National Period), schools
  and groups (Lake School, Bloomsbury Group, Metaphysical Poets), movements
  (naturalism, existentialism).
- About 6 prize items: Nobel and Pulitzer recipients, sometimes with the
  citation quoted, and author-identification items (Harper Lee, Steinbeck,
  Elizabeth Barrett Browning, Shakespeare).
- Prize items draw only from the year windows for that meet. See
  `reading-list/2026-2027.md`.

Part 2 (20 items): split by work, roughly five drama, seven fiction, eight
poetry. Two poems printed with line numbers, four items each, asking for
the device in a cited line. Fiction and drama items ask who said what, to
whom, where, and the device in a quoted sentence.

Part 3 (15 items): three or four unseen poems (in 2026: Wilfred Owen,
Shelley, Mark Strand, plus a senryu and a clerihew). Items ask for the
governing device, the form, the rhyme type, the theme stated as a sentence,
and which lines carry an idea.

The answer key cites Handbook 12e page numbers for nearly every item, so
a student who misses an item can look up the exact definition UIL used.

## Where the points come from

Parts 2 and 3 are 70 of the 100 points. A student who knows the reading
list cold and reads unseen passages carefully can outscore a student who
has memorized the glossary. Order of investment:

1. Reading list (Part 2). Highest yield, fully under the student's control.
2. Unseen-passage skills (Part 3). Trainable with weekly practice, and the
   device vocabulary overlaps heavily with Part 2's poetry items.
3. Terms and history (Part 1). The 221 terms tested five or more times
   since 2009 cover about two thirds of Part 1 term items
   (`terms/most-tested-terms.md`). Learn those first, then the glossary.

## Time plan for students

- Part 1: about 15 minutes. Mark and move on.
- Part 2: about 20 minutes.
- Part 3: about 30 minutes. Read each passage twice before the items.
- Essay: the remaining 20-25 minutes. Never skip it.
- Fill every bubble before time is called.

## Part 3 habits worth drilling

- Identify the form first (sonnet, blank verse, free verse, fixed form).
- Name the speaker and situation before naming devices.
- For meter and rhyme items, scan the line on paper.
- Eliminate options that describe something not in the passage.
- Read the stem for which lines it points to; many items cite lines.

## Advancement `[verify in Section 940]`

- Individual: top three advance from District to Region and from Region
  to State.
- Team: top three individual scores form the team score; the first-place
  team advances, with wild cards by score.

# UIL Literary Criticism: format, scoring, strategy

Verify against the current Literary Criticism Handbook and Section 940 of
the UIL Constitution & Contest Rules each season. Items marked `[verify]`
are from memory or secondary sources.

## The exam

90 minutes. 65 multiple-choice items (five options each) and one essay.

| Part | What it tests | Items | Points each | Max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Literary terms and literary history, incl. ~8 Nobel/Pulitzer items | 30 | 1 | 30 |
| 2 | The annual reading list (novel, drama, poems) | 20 | 2 | 40 |
| 3 | Ability in literary criticism: unseen passages | 15 | 2 | 30 |
| 4 | Tie-breaker essay on a short passage | 1 | 0 | 0 |

Maximum score 100. There is no deduction for wrong answers `[verify]`, so
every item should be answered.

The essay is read only to break ties. Judges compare essays on, in order:
response to the prompt, quality and depth of the literary analysis, and
quality of written expression.

## Advancement

- Individual places: top six at each meet score; the top three individuals
  advance from District to Regional and from Regional to State `[verify]`.
- Team: the top three individual scores from a school form the team score.
  The first-place team advances, and wild-card teams may advance based on
  score `[verify]`.

## Where the points come from

Parts 2 and 3 are 70 of the 100 points. A student who knows the reading
list cold and reads unseen passages carefully can outscore a student who
has memorized the glossary. Order of investment:

1. Reading list (Part 2). Highest yield, fully under the student's control.
2. Unseen-passage skills (Part 3). Trainable with weekly practice.
3. Terms and history (Part 1). Broad and long-tail; worth steady effort but
   the last point is expensive.

## Time plan for students

- Part 1: about 15 minutes. Do not get stuck; mark and move on.
- Part 2: about 20 minutes. This is where preparation pays.
- Part 3: about 30 minutes. Read the passage twice before the items.
- Essay: the remaining 20-25 minutes. Plan for two minutes, write for the
  rest, leave one minute to fix the ending.
- Fill every bubble before time is called.

## Part 3 habits worth drilling

- Identify the form first (sonnet? blank verse? prose? dramatic speech?).
- Name the speaker and the situation before the devices.
- For meter and rhyme items, scan the line on paper.
- Eliminate options that describe something not in the passage.
- Watch for items that ask about the whole passage vs. one line.

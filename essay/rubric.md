# Tie-breaker essay rubric

The essay is required. From the test instructions: contestants who do not
write an essay are disqualified even if they are not involved in a tie, and
an essay that does not show a sincere effort to discuss the assigned topic
is disqualified. Three sheets of paper are provided; the expected length is
a "moderately brief discussion of a restricted topic."

UIL's criteria for judges, in order:

1. The degree to which the instructions have been followed.
2. The quality of the critical insight offered in response to the selection.
3. The overall effectiveness of the written discussion.
4. The grammatical correctness of the essay.

UIL's note: critical insight matters more than prose style, and the
objective score is never changed by the essay; only tie rankings are.

## Practice scoring, 1-4 per criterion (16 = model)

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Follows the instructions | off-topic or summary only | touches the prompt | answers the prompt directly | answers precisely, every paragraph serves the prompt |
| Critical insight | names devices without effect | explains one effect | connects devices to meaning with quoted evidence | sustained close reading; shows how form makes meaning; uses Handbook terms correctly |
| Effectiveness of the discussion | hard to follow | clear but thin | organized and developed | economical; each paragraph advances the claim |
| Grammatical correctness | errors obscure meaning | frequent errors | few errors | clean |

## The shape of a prompt

The 2026 Invitational A prompt printed Shakespeare's Sonnet 34 with glosses
and asked: "discuss the speaker's change in attitude to whom he is speaking
as it appears in the closing couplet." The judges' notes listed the terms
a strong essay might use (apostrophe, volta, rhetorical question, tone,
epiphany, metaphor, chiasmus, sigmatism, diction, rhyme scheme) and the
reading they expected: a logical turn bordering on emotional in the
couplet, an implied epiphany in "Ah," and forgiveness framed by the tears
as pearls.

So the prompt names one restricted thing (a change, a device, a line's
effect). The essay answers that one thing.

## Twenty-minute plan

1. Read the passage twice. Underline the three details you will use.
2. One-sentence claim that answers the prompt using its own words.
3. Three short paragraphs: one detail each, quote it, name the device in
   Handbook terms, say what it does.
4. One-sentence close that returns to the claim.
5. No introduction paragraph; start with the claim.

## Common failures

- Retelling the passage.
- Listing devices without connecting any to the prompt.
- Answering a broader question than the one asked.
- Running out of time before the ending, or not writing at all.

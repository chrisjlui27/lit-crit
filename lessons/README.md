# Lessons

UIL's sample lesson plans are in `resources/UIL-LitCrit-sample-lessons.pdf`
(five lessons with TEKS alignment and worksheets). The parts that fit a
ten-minute q-time block are summarized here so they can be run without
the PDF.

## 1. Close reading for fiction (Hawthorne)

Six headings; ask one or two per session on the assigned story.

1. First impressions: what the title suggests, the mood, and whether the
   first things noticed complement or contradict each other.
2. Language: the first words that stand out, unusual choices, double
   meanings, unfamiliar words.
3. Patterns: repeated images, connections to elsewhere in the story,
   whether the passage is a microcosm of the whole, sentence rhythm,
   punctuation, what is left out.
4. Point of view and characterization: who speaks, to whom, limited or
   omniscient, sensory detail and why it was chosen.
5. Symbolism: metaphors, a controlling metaphor, objects standing for
   something else, traditional or biblical connotations, allegory.
6. Theme: the assertion the story makes.

## 2. Close reading for poetry (Bradstreet)

Four headings, from the Draine explication method in the PDF.

1. Situation: narrative or lyric, speaker and persona, addressee, tone,
   theme.
2. Structure: form and line count, movement (chronological, cause and
   effect, circling back, a turn), syntax, punctuation (end-stopped vs.
   enjambed, mid-line pauses), title.
3. Language: diction (colloquial, formal), allusions, imagery and figures.
4. Music: rhyme scheme, meter and its tonal effect, alliteration,
   assonance, consonance.

## 3. Poetic devices (writing to learn)

The PDF's twenty-term glossary (alliteration, allusion, anastrophe,
apostrophe, assonance, cacophony, caesura, consonance, couplet, euphony,
hyperbole, litotes, metaphor, onomatopoeia, oxymoron, personification,
simile, synecdoche, syllepsis, tautology) with two worksheets: students
write lines that contain named devices ("a line with a caesura and
alliteration"; "two lines with a couplet and litotes"). Good Monday filler
when a terms batch is short: two minutes, write one line, read it aloud.

## 4. Meter and scansion

Handout lists iambic, trochaic, anapestic, dactylic, spondaic, and pyrrhic
feet with example lines, and monometer through octameter. Worksheet: scan
four excerpts (Frank Jacobs, Keats, Byron, Dickinson) and name the
pattern. One excerpt per Thursday fits in ten minutes.

## 5. Sonnets

Italian (octave abbaabba, sestet in several patterns, never ending in a
couplet), Spenserian (abab bcbc cdcd ee), Shakespearean (abab cdcd efef
gg). Worksheet: mark meter and rhyme scheme and identify the type for
Shakespeare 130, Sidney 71, and Spenser 54. Sonnet-type items are among
the most tested Part 1 terms (Italian sonnet, Miltonic sonnet,
Shakespearean sonnet, volta), so run this in October.
